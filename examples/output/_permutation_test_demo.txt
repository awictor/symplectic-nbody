Permutation test: is the gap real, or just how the labels fell?

  small trial (exact enumeration of all label assignments):
    treated mean 5.72, control mean 3.50, observed diff 2.220
    enumerated 126 permutations -> exact p = 0.0079
    significant at 0.05

  larger trial (Monte-Carlo, t-statistic, 20000 shuffles):
    group A mean 10.41, group B mean 11.80
    observed t = -2.327, p = 0.0227 (Monte-Carlo)

  paired before/after (sign-flip, exact over 2**n sign patterns):
    mean drop 1.762 across 8 subjects
    enumerated 256 sign patterns -> p = 0.0078
    significant at 0.05

  The p-value IS the fraction of relabellings at least as extreme as what we saw. Under
  the null the labels are exchangeable, so this fraction is the true tail probability --
  exact when enumerated, Monte-Carlo estimated with the (b+1)/(B+1) correction otherwise.
