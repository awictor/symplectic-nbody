VCG: the auction where honesty is the dominant strategy

  single-item second-price (Vickrey) auction, bids [12, 20, 15, 8]:
    bidder 1 wins, pays the second-highest bid = 15, surplus 5

  combinatorial VCG: assign ['top', 'mid', 'low'] to ['Acme', 'Bolt', 'Cog']
    value matrix (advertiser x slot):
             top   mid   low
       Acme   30    18     6
       Bolt   24    20    10
        Cog   15    14    12

    efficient allocation (maximises total value = 62):
       Acme: top           pays   24  (externality on others), utility    6
       Bolt: mid           pays   18  (externality on others), utility    2
        Cog: low           pays   10  (externality on others), utility    2

  can Acme do better by lying about its values?
    truthful utility 6; best utility over tried lies 6
    -> lying never beats the truth: True

  VCG picks the value-maximising allocation, then charges each winner the harm it does to
  everyone else (its externality). Because your report only changes WHETHER you win, not what
  you pay for a given outcome, truth-telling is a dominant strategy -- the principle behind
  spectrum auctions and the second-price ad exchanges that run the web.
