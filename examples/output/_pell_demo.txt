Pell's equation x^2 - D y^2 = 1, via the continued fraction of sqrt(D)

  fundamental solutions (smallest x, y > 0):
       D         cf period               x             y
       2             [1;2]               3             2
       3           [1;1,2]               2             1
       5             [2;4]               9             4

      13     [3;1,1,1,1,6]             649           180

      46[6;1,3,1,1,2,6,2,1,1,3,1,12]           24335          3588
      61[7;1,4,3,1,2,2,1,3,4,1,14]      1766319049     226153980

  Fermat's challenge D=61: x = 1766319049, y = 226153980
  verify: 1766319049^2 - 61*226153980^2 = 1

  negative Pell x^2 - D y^2 = -1 exists iff sqrt(D)'s CF period is odd:
    D= 2 (period odd): (1, 1) -> -1
    D= 5 (period odd): (2, 1) -> -1
    D=10 (period odd): (3, 1) -> -1
    D=13 (period odd): (18, 5) -> -1
    D= 3 (period even): no solution
    D= 7 (period even): no solution

  successive solutions for D=2 grow exponentially:
    solution 1: (3, 2)
    solution 2: (17, 12)
    solution 3: (99, 70)
    solution 4: (577, 408)
    solution 5: (3363, 2378)
    solution 6: (19601, 13860)

  The digit-length of the fundamental x jumps chaotically with D -- D=61 needs 10
  digits while its neighbours need 2 or 3. This erratic growth is why Pell is hard.
