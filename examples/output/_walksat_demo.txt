WalkSAT: solving SAT by flipping variables to repair conflicts

  small formula (x1 v x2 v ~x3) ^ (~x1 v x3) ^ ...:
    solved: True, model: x1=T, x2=T, x3=T, verified: True

  random 3-SAT: 40 variables, 160 clauses (ratio 4.0)
    DPLL says satisfiable: True
    WalkSAT found a model: True (verified)

  conflict trajectory: started with 20 unsatisfied clauses, reached 0 at best
    each flip picks a random unsatisfied clause and flips one of its variables -- greedily
    (fewest clauses broken) most of the time, randomly sometimes to escape local minima.

  WalkSAT is INCOMPLETE: it can't prove unsatisfiability, only find models. But on large
  satisfiable instances -- where DPLL's backtracking tree explodes -- it often lands a
  solution almost instantly, which is why stochastic local search underpins the fastest
  incomplete SAT solvers.
