Sums of squares: Fermat's two-square theorem and Lagrange's four-square theorem

  primes as sums of two squares (Fermat: p = 2 or p = 1 mod 4):
     prime  mod 4       a^2 + b^2
         2      2   1^2 + 1^2 = 2
         5      1   2^2 + 1^2 = 5
        13      1   3^2 + 2^2 = 13
        17      1   4^2 + 1^2 = 17
        29      1   5^2 + 2^2 = 29
        37      1   6^2 + 1^2 = 37
        41      1   5^2 + 4^2 = 41
         3      3   (not a sum of two squares)
         7      3   (not a sum of two squares)
        11      3   (not a sum of two squares)
        19      3   (not a sum of two squares)

  Cornacchia + Brahmagupta-Fibonacci build composite representations:
    325 = 1^2 + 18^2 = 325
    1105 = 9^2 + 32^2 = 1105
    5525 = 14^2 + 73^2 = 5525

  Lagrange: EVERY integer is a sum of four squares (the 4^a(8b+7) cases need all four):
    7 = 1^2 + 1^2 + 1^2 + 2^2  (4 nonzero squares)
    15 = 1^2 + 1^2 + 2^2 + 3^2  (4 nonzero squares)
    23 = 2^2 + 1^2 + 3^2 + 3^2  (4 nonzero squares)
    31 = 1^2 + 1^2 + 2^2 + 5^2  (4 nonzero squares)
    127 = 1^2 + 1^2 + 2^2 + 11^2  (4 nonzero squares)
    28 = 1^2 + 1^2 + 1^2 + 5^2  (4 nonzero squares)
