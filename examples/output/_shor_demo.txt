Shor's algorithm: factoring by quantum period-finding

  factor N = 21, base a = 2
  f(x) = 2^x mod 21 is periodic; its period is the order of 2 mod 21

  2^x mod 21:  [1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 11, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 11]
  period r = 6  (since 2^6 mod 21 = 1)

  QFT over 128 states: probability peaks at multiples of 128/6 = 21:
    peaks at [0, 21, 43, 64, 85, 107]

  r is even, so compute a^(r/2) = 2^3 mod 21 = 8
  gcd(8-1, 21) = 7,  gcd(8+1, 21) = 3

  Shor's algorithm result: 21 = 7 x 3

  The exponential speed-up is entirely in finding r: classically hard, but the QFT
  reads the period off the interference pattern above in one shot. This is why a large
  quantum computer would break RSA.
