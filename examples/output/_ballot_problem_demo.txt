Bertrand's ballot problem -- P(candidate A leads the entire count) = (p-q)/(p+q)
==============================================================================

A finishes with p votes, B with q < p. Counted in random order, how often is A
strictly ahead at every step? Depends only on the margin relative to turnout:

     p     q    margin    P(lead throughout)   good orderings / all
   --------------------------------------------------------------
      3     2       1        0.2000               2 / 10    
      5     3       2        0.2500              14 / 56    
     10     8       2        0.1111            4862 / 43758 
      6     1       5        0.7143               5 / 7     
      8     2       6        0.6000              27 / 45    
     10     0      10        1.0000               1 / 1     

A landslide is almost never behind; a dead heat (p=q+1) leads throughout only 1/(p+q).

Three independent derivations of the strict count agree exactly:
   (p,q)    formula   reflection   cycle-lemma   brute-force
   --------------------------------------------------------
   (4,2)         5         5           5             5
   (5,3)        14        14          14            14
   (6,2)        14        14          14            14
   (7,4)        90        90          90            90

At p = q (weak lead, ties allowed) the count IS the Catalan numbers -- Dyck paths:
   catalan(0..8) = 1, 1, 2, 5, 14, 42, 132, 429, 1430
