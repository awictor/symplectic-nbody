FitzHugh-Nagumo -- the two-variable cartoon of a spiking neuron
==========================================================================

  dv/dt = v - v^3/3 - w + I     (fast voltage-like variable)
  dw/dt = eps (v + a - b w)     (slow recovery, eps << 1)

At rest (I=0): fixed point (v*,w*) = (-1.199, -0.624), stable = True.

Threshold excitability -- a kick must clear the middle branch of the cubic to spike:
   kick to v      peak v reached     spike?
   --------------------------------------------
   -1.099         -1.099          decays
   -0.899         -0.899          decays
   -0.699         -0.670          decays
   -0.399         +1.771          SPIKE
   +0.301         +1.827          SPIKE

Small kicks decay straight back; past threshold the cubic amplifies the kick into a
full ~2-unit spike before the slow recovery drags v home. No switch is hard-coded.

Inject steady current I -> Hopf bifurcation -> repetitive firing (a relaxation oscillator):
   fixed point is unstable for I in (0.335, 1.415) -- two Hopf points.
   I        fixed pt      behaviour
   ----------------------------------------
   0.00     stable        rest
   0.20     stable        rest
   0.35     UNSTABLE      repetitive spiking
   0.60     UNSTABLE      repetitive spiking
   1.00     UNSTABLE      repetitive spiking
   1.40     UNSTABLE      repetitive spiking
   1.80     stable        rest

Below and above the window the neuron rests; inside it fires forever -- the excitable
cell has become an oscillator. Same equations, current is the only knob.
