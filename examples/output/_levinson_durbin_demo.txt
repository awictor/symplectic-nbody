Levinson-Durbin: O(n^2) Toeplitz solving and AR modelling

  Toeplitz system T x = b, T built from first row [4,1,0]:
    b = [1.0, 2.0, 3.0]  ->  x = [0.1786, 0.2857, 0.6786]
    matrix positive definite? True

  AR(2) fit on 4000 samples of x_t = 0.75 x_{t-1} - 0.5 x_{t-2} + noise:
    true coeffs      [0.75, -0.50]
    recovered coeffs [0.7353, -0.4991]
    reflection coeffs [-0.4905, 0.4991] (all |k|<1 -> stable)
    prediction-error variance 0.0916 (noise var ~ 0.0900)

  5-step forecast from the fitted model:
    [-0.4062, -0.0548, 0.1625, 0.1468, 0.0269]

  A stationary signal has a Toeplitz covariance matrix, so its Yule-Walker normal
  equations are Toeplitz and Levinson-Durbin solves them in O(n^2) instead of O(n^3),
  yielding the AR coefficients that LPC, spectral estimation, and forecasting all use.
