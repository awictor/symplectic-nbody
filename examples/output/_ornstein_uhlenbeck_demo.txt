Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process -- the mean-reverting random walk
==========================================================================

  dX = theta (mu - X) dt + sigma dW      (spring pull to mu + white noise)
  theta=1.0 (rate)   mu=1.0 (mean)   sigma=0.6 (noise)

Exact solvability -- every moment in closed form:
  correlation time 1/theta = 1.000
  stationary law: Normal(mu=1.0, var=sigma^2/2theta=0.1800)

Relaxation from x0=4 toward mu=1 -- Monte Carlo (6000 paths) tracks the analytic curves:
    t      E[X_t] analytic   MC mean     Var analytic   MC var
   ------------------------------------------------------------
    0.2       3.4562        3.4577      0.0593       0.0590
    0.5       2.8196        2.8207      0.1138       0.1133
    1.0       2.1036        2.1046      0.1556       0.1542
    2.0       1.4060        1.4075      0.1767       0.1799
    4.0       1.0549        1.0551      0.1799       0.1839

Mean decays exponentially to mu; variance climbs from 0 to the stationary value.

Exact Gaussian stepper has NO time-step error; Euler-Maruyama is biased at large dt:
    dt      analytic Var(dt)   exact stepper   Euler-Maruyama
   ----------------------------------------------------------
    0.1         0.0326          0.0327         0.0361
    0.5         0.1138          0.1140         0.1803
    1.0         0.1556          0.1559         0.3606
    2.0         0.1767          0.1770         0.7211

Euler injects sigma^2*dt of variance per step regardless of relaxation -> overshoots badly.
The exact stepper matches truth at any dt because the OU transition is Gaussian.
