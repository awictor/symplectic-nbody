Quaternions: gimbal-lock-free rotation and smooth interpolation

  composing rotations (apply yaw 90 then pitch 90 to the x-axis):
    after yaw 90:        (+0.000, +1.000, +0.000)
    after pitch 90 too:  (+0.000, +1.000, +0.000)

  representation round trip (Euler 30/45/60 deg):
    -> quaternion (+0.822, +0.022, +0.440, +0.360)
    -> back to Euler 30.0/45.0/60.0 deg

  slerp vs component-lerp over a 170-degree turn (angle from start at each step):
    t      slerp    lerp
    0.0      0.0      0.0
    0.2     34.0     27.4
    0.4     68.0     64.2
    0.6    102.0    105.8
    0.8    136.0    142.6
    1.0    170.0    170.0
    (slerp increments evenly; lerp lags then rushes -- uneven angular velocity)

  A unit quaternion (cos(theta/2), sin(theta/2)*axis) encodes a rotation; composing them is
  one multiply, and slerp walks the great-circle arc at constant speed. Four numbers, one
  unit-length constraint, no gimbal lock -- why aerospace and graphics run on quaternions.
