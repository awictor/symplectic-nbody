Dancing Links (DLX): exact cover by Algorithm X

  Knuth's example -- cover items 1..7 with disjoint options:
    A: [1, 4, 7]
    B: [1, 4]
    C: [4, 5, 7]
    D: [3, 5, 6]
    E: [2, 3, 6, 7]
    F: [2, 7]
    exact cover: ['B', 'D', 'F']  (options ['B', 'D', 'F'] partition 1..7 exactly)

  N-queens as exact cover (place n non-attacking queens):
    1-queens: 1 solution(s)
    2-queens: 0 solution(s)
    3-queens: 0 solution(s)
    4-queens: 2 solution(s)
    5-queens: 10 solution(s)
    6-queens: 4 solution(s)
    7-queens: 40 solution(s)
    8-queens: 92 solution(s)

  one 6-queens solution (Q = queen):
    . Q . . . .
    . . . Q . .
    . . . . . Q
    Q . . . . .
    . . Q . . .
    . . . . Q .

  DLX stores the cover matrix as a toroidal doubly-linked list; 'covering' a column
  splices it and its conflicting options out with x.L.R=x.R; x.R.L=x.L, and 'uncovering'
  on backtrack restores them with the exact inverse -- no allocation during the search,
  and the pointers literally dance out and back. The column-with-fewest-options heuristic
  keeps the branching minimal.
